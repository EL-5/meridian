          Start here

          This app tells you when the forex market is busy, and which pairs are most active. It does not tell you whether to buy or sell, or whether now is a good moment to trade.

          The market never sleeps, but it does get quiet

          Forex is traded around the clock from Monday to Friday. It follows the sun across four financial centres: Sydney, Tokyo, London and New York. When more centres are open, more people are trading. More people trading usually means lower costs and more movement.

          The five levels

          The big word on the Now tab is one of five levels. Nothing here predicts whether a trade will win. The levels describe how busy the market is and how much it costs to trade.

          LevelWhat is openWhat it means
          PeakLondon and New YorkBusiest time. Lowest costs. Prices move the most, and losses can come as fast as wins.
          GoodLondon, or Tokyo and LondonPlenty of activity. Costs are low.
          SteadyOnly Tokyo, or only New YorkSlower. Prices move less.
          QuietOnly Sydney, or nothingFew traders. Costs are higher. Many people wait.
          ClosedWeekendNo trading until Sunday evening.

          A simple way to use it

          1. Look at the big word. If it says Peak or Good, activity is high. If it says Quiet, many traders wait.
          2. Look at "Most active pairs now". These are the pairs with the most activity right now. Each one has a short note on what it is like. Busy is not the same as safe.
          3. Read any amber warning. It appears for bank holidays, the daily rollover at 5 pm New York, Wednesday's triple swap, the weekend close, and when today is running busier or quieter than the clock suggests. Costs rise and prices can jump.
          4. Pick one session to study. Tap a session in the list to see how it is going: whether it is busier or quieter than usual.

          If you trade on Exness

          • Your MetaTrader clock. Exness says its MT4 and MT5 platforms run on GMT+0 (UTC+0) and the time cannot be changed. This app is set up for that, so chart times match the Exness terminal.
          • TradingView is different. Its clock follows the timezone you choose for the chart. In TradingView, right-click the time axis and pick UTC so it matches Exness.
          • Daily and 4-hour candles on MT4 and MT5 are cut by server time, so they can look different from the same candles on TradingView.
          • Market week. The Now tab shows when the Exness forex week closes on Friday and reopens on Sunday. These come from Exness's published schedule, which moves by one hour with New York daylight saving. Confirm the exact times for your account in the Exness app.
